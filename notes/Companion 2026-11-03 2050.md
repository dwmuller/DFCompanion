# Companion toolset review — session 254-08-06 (handoff doc for dev strategist)

Context for the reader: read-only DFHack-backed toolset, 20 tools, used across two turns to (a) open the fortress, (b) answer "who should join a full-time melee squad" and "is the fort sealed". Everything below is grounded in queries actually run this session.

## What works well (keep this shape)

- **`find_candidates` is the best-designed tool in the set.** Filters + ranking + compact per-match evidence, one call to go from "111 dwarves" to "nine plausible soldiers" with skills, mood, health and squad status inline. This is the pattern to generalize.
- **`get_strange_moods`** — demands with delivered-counts, claimed workshop, days-to-insanity. Exactly the right abstraction level; no raw data dumping.
- **`get_food_drink_summary`** — precomputes the thing the player cares about (days of supply, variety, rotten/forbidden split) instead of listing items.
- **`get_housing`** — summary + per-floor counts + paged unhoused list answered a real question in one call.
- **Descriptions are honest about scope**, especially the "configuration, not inferred effective labor permissions" and "untrusted data" caveats. That prevented two wrong inferences on my side.
- **Position format `(x,y,z)` with game-UI z** is consistent across every tool. Cheap consistency win.

## P0 — omissions that block whole classes of questions

**1. No map/tile access. This is the single biggest gap.**
The player asserted the fort is sealed against the cave swallow men. I had positions (x84–91, z −75/−76) and hostile dispositions, and *no way to verify pathability*: no walls, doors, hatches, ramps, stairs, or lock/forbid state on constructions. Every spatial recommendation (where a barracks goes, whether a stockpile is reachable, whether that z45 dorm is behind a hatch) is unfalsifiable guesswork.
Suggested minimal surface, in priority order:
- `pathable(from, to) → bool` (or path length) — one call, enormous leverage.
- `tiles_at(position, radius)` or `describe_position` — what occupies this tile (construction, item stack count, creature, zone, web/blood/lava).
- `list_constructions(type?)` — doors/hatches/floodgates with **lock state and forbidden flag**.
Even a coarse pathability + door-lock query turns "I'm confident" into "confirmed/unconfirmed".

**2. No item-instance view; only aggregates.**
I could count "10 iron spears, 2 copper battle axes, 1 blistered-metal scimitar" and "10 wooden shields", but not quality, material of each shield, wear, weight, or **whether a given dwarf could actually be assigned one**. The uniform logic in `list_squads` reports *what is missing* but not *why* — a plausible cause it cannot express: "eligible item exists but is forbidden / in a far stockpile / claimed by another squad".
Suggested: `list_items` with item-type filter returning per-instance quality, material, wear, forbidden, container, holder/assignment; plus `equipment_shortfall(squad) → {slot, candidates[], blocking_reason}`. Without this, "can I equip nine dwarves" is answerable only in counts.

## P1 — needed for the strategy the player is actually pursuing

**3. Military is half-modeled.** Uniform *targets* per member and missing slots are exposed; missing entirely: the squad's **uniform template** (which item types/materials are wanted), **schedule/routine orders** (train/station/patrol with months and minimums), **alert states and civilian alert**, **station points**, and **burrow assignment per unit** (also relevant civically). I recommended "make this a full-time melee squad" while unable to see whether the existing routine already was one, beyond a free-text "Constant training".

**4. No labor-economics aggregate; absence-of-skill is invisible.** Advising on recruitment moved seven named specialists into a squad; I had to hand-check, per skill, whether each was a single point of failure (only butcher, best armorsmith, only gem setter). The toolset made that a manual multi-query exercise. `list_work_details` documents it is configuration only, so there is also **no effective-labor resolution** — "who will do weaponsmithing in the next hour" is unanswerable.
Suggested: `skill_coverage(min_level)` → skill / holder count / names / on-fire status; and `labor_impact(unit, new_role)` → what trades go unstaffed if this dwarf is reassigned. That second one is the highest-value *derived* query in the whole set.

**5. Noble mandates, room values, and happiness drivers.** I could see noble *positions* but not mandates/demands (the hammering risk), not room value vs requirement, and not a per-dwarf "what would fix this mood" mapping beyond unmet-need labels with numbers like −84540. A `happiness_drivers(unit)` that translates each unmet need into an actionable object ("ýnul worship: nearest temple zone id, any idol available") plus a relationship graph (spouse/parent/child) would make stress advice concrete rather than decorative.

## P2 — quantitative logistics and history

**6. No distance/travel-time metric.** Positions exist everywhere; distances don't. Stockpile-to-workshop, patient-to-hospital, well-to-morgue analysis needs path length, not Euclidean guesswork.
**7. No farm/crop/seed production model.** `get_building_counts` counts farm plots; nothing reports plot dimensions, current crop, season, seed stock, or production rate — so food-supply advice can only ever be about existing stock, which is the easy half.
**8. No history/legends layer.** Dead/missing gives a date and no cause, killer, or circumstances; no kill lists, no artifact registry (artifacts inside inventories or displayed furniture are invisible to `search_items`). "58 dead or missing on record" is a number with no story)
