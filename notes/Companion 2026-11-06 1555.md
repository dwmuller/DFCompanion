## Highest-value gaps, in order

**1. **2. No fortress-level labor coverage query.** Nothing answers "which labors have zero capable workers" or "how many residents have labor L". I rebuilt that by hand from `list_work_details` + `get_dfhack_automation` + job strings. A derived endpoint (labor → enabled count, current-doer count) would have replaced most of this conversation with one call. This is likely the app's single most-requested feature.

**3. No reverse item lookup.** `search_items` explicitly excludes carried items, and inventory is only reachable per-unit. So "who holds the 10 iron spears?" is a manual sweep of 111 units. For uniform and armour planning that join is the whole question. Related: `search_items` gives at most 3 sample positions, so "where are my 91 iron bars" is a partial answer.

**4. No reachability or pathing data.** Accepting your point on the swallows — but flagging that nothing in the toolset could confirm *or* deny it. There's no connectivity, burrow-access, or edge-to-fort-path query, so any "they can't get in" claim is unfalsifiable from data. Probably expensive to add honestly; a cheap proxy would be flagging creatures whose agenda targets a fortress position.

**5. Military schedule is invisible.** `list_squads` gives routine, uniforms, weapons, wear — no schedule months, no training-ground/barracks assignment, no squad-level uniform definition. Your autotraining note is the perfect illustration: without `get_dfhack_automation` reporting autotraining on, squad-membership churn looks like a bug.
